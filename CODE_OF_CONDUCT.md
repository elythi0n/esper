# Code of conduct

## The short version

Be decent. Assume the person you are talking to is trying to make this
better. Say what you mean about the work, not about the person doing it.

## What is expected

- **Talk about the code.** "This drops frames on a 4K screen" is useful.
  "This is amateurish" is not, and says nothing a reader can act on.
- **Bring the evidence.** This project's habit is to measure rather than
  assert, and that applies to disagreements too: a number, a repro, or a
  quoted line settles more than a strong opinion.
- **Accept a decision that went the other way.** Most choices here are
  written down with their reasons ([`docs/`](docs)), including the ones that
  were changed twice. Reopening one is fine; reopening one without addressing
  the reason is not.
- **Take security seriously, and privately.** A vulnerability goes to the
  address in [`SECURITY.md`](SECURITY.md), not into an issue.

## What is not

Harassment, in the ordinary meaning of the word: personal attacks, slurs,
sexual attention nobody asked for, deliberate misgendering, following
somebody from thread to thread, publishing private information, or making
this an unpleasant place to be on purpose.

It applies in the issue tracker, in pull requests, in commit messages and in
any other space this project uses — and in private messages that follow from
one of those.

## Reporting

Email **conduct@elythion.com**. Say what happened, where, and who was
involved; include links if there are any.

Reports are read by the maintainers. You will get an acknowledgement, and you
will be told what was decided. What you send is not shared with the person
reported without asking you first — with one exception, stated plainly
because it would otherwise be a surprise: if acting on a report requires
saying who made it, you will be asked, and you may say no and have the report
handled on what can be acted on without it.

If a report concerns a maintainer, it can be sent to any other maintainer,
and that person will handle it alone.

## What happens

In rough order, and not a ladder that must be climbed a rung at a time — the
first response to something serious can be the last one:

1. A private word, saying which behaviour and why.
2. A public correction on the thread, so people reading it later are not left
   thinking it went unanswered.
3. A temporary block from the project's spaces.
4. A permanent one.

Nothing here is a promise to litigate every disagreement. A maintainer may
close a thread that has stopped being about the work, and that is not a
finding against anybody in it.

## Scope, honestly

This is a small project. There is no committee, no appeals board and no
process document behind this one. What is here is what the maintainers will
actually do, written down so nobody has to guess.
